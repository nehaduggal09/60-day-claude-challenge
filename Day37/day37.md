# 🧭 Task Compass

### Learn how work flows through real organizations.

**Task Compass** is an interactive workplace simulation designed to help learners understand how real organizations handle tasks, responsibilities, communication, and collaboration.

Instead of testing job titles or memorized definitions, the experience teaches **ownership, delegation, workflow thinking, escalation, and collaboration** through realistic workplace situations.

---

## 🎯 Objective

The goal of Task Compass is to help players understand:

* Who should own a task
* When and how work should be delegated
* How different roles collaborate
* How tasks move through an organization
* When escalation or cross-team communication is needed
* Why complex workplace problems are rarely solved by one person alone

---

## 🎮 Gameplay

Task Compass contains **three progressive stages**.

### Stage 1 — Who Owns This?

Players receive realistic workplace tasks and choose the most appropriate primary owner from a collection of role cards.

Each scenario encourages players to think about:

* Primary ownership
* Supporting roles
* Responsibility boundaries
* Why a particular role is best positioned to lead

The game provides an explanation after every decision rather than simply marking answers as right or wrong.

### Stage 2 — Task Routing

Players become responsible for routing incoming work through the organization.

They drag role cards into a logical workflow, such as:

```text
Customer Support
        ↓
       QA
        ↓
Backend Developer
        ↓
Product Manager
        ↓
    Customer
```

After submission, the workflow is animated to demonstrate how work can move between teams.

### Stage 3 — Collaboration Challenge

Players face larger organizational situations such as:

* Customer satisfaction suddenly drops
* Sales increase dramatically
* Negative reviews appear online
* A major feature is delayed

Instead of selecting a single owner, players can assign up to **four roles or departments**.

The game then explains:

* Primary Owner
* Supporting Teams
* Reasoning
* Possible Communication Flow

This emphasizes collaborative problem solving.

## 📊 Understanding Categories

Instead of traditional points, Task Compass measures four areas of understanding:

| Category             | What it represents                                   |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| 👤 Ownership         | Understanding who should lead a task                 |
| 🔄 Delegation        | Understanding how responsibility can be distributed  |
| 🤝 Collaboration     | Recognizing when multiple teams are needed           |
| 🧭 Workflow Thinking | Understanding how work moves through an organization |

A visual radar/bar-style summary is displayed at the end.

---

## 🪞 Final Reflection

The experience ends with a reflection instead of a conventional score.

Players receive insights such as:

* What they understood well
* Where they tended to over-assign responsibility
* Where they underestimated collaboration
* An insight about how organizations actually work

The reflection focuses on learning rather than judging performance.

## 🎨 Design Philosophy

Task Compass is designed to feel more like a **strategy game** than an examination.

The interface combines:

* Dark visual language
* Glass-style panels
* Department-specific colors
* Rounded interactive cards
* Smooth transitions
* Drag-and-drop interactions
* Progress feedback
* Animated workflows

The objective is to make organizational learning feel **interactive, visual, and engaging**.

---

## 🧠 Learning Philosophy

Real workplace problems often involve multiple layers of responsibility.

A task may have:

```text
Primary Owner
      ↓
Supporting Team
      ↓
Specialist / Reviewer
      ↓
Decision Maker
      ↓
Customer / Stakeholder
```

Task Compass helps learners recognize that **ownership does not always mean doing everything yourself**.

Effective organizations often depend on clear ownership combined with communication, delegation, and collaboration.
